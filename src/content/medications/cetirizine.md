Cetirizine occupies the histamine H1 receptor, preventing histamine already released from mast cells from producing its downstream effects — vasodilation, increased vascular permeability, pruritus, and sensory nerve stimulation. It acts on the mediator's target rather than on the cell, so it does nothing to reduce degranulation itself.

Its randomised evidence comes from chronic spontaneous urticaria and allergic rhinitis rather than from MCAS. H1 blockade is the first step in the stepwise mediator-blockade approach described in the mast cell disease management literature.
