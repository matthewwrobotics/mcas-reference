Hydroxyzine is a first-generation H1 receptor antagonist. It occupies the receptor that histamine would activate after mast-cell release, so its placement here is downstream of the mast cell rather than as a mast-cell stabiliser.

The current US label lists histamine-mediated pruritus, including chronic urticaria, among its uses. The mast-cell-disease trial compared hydroxyzine, ketotifen, and placebo in eight children with mastocytosis using a crossover design. Its age, size, and population make it a narrow evidence source rather than an MCAS trial.

Unlike less-sedating second-generation H1 antihistamines, hydroxyzine readily produces central nervous system effects. The current label’s drowsiness, CNS-depressant interaction, and cardiac-rhythm warnings are practical distinctions that should remain visible even though they do not change the evidence grade.
