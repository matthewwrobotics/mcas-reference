Cromolyn sodium inhibits the release of preformed mediators from mast cell granules, and is the compound from which the "mast cell stabiliser" class takes its name — it descends from khellin, isolated from *Ammi visnaga*. The precise molecular target remains contested; reviews of the class note that cromolyn's stabilising effect was characterised clinically well before its mechanism was, and that several proposed targets have not been settled.

A 2026 retrospective case series described five people who also had ME/CFS and met consensus-2 MCAS criteria. The patients were not a random or consecutive sample, there was no comparator, symptoms and wellness were self-reported, assessment varied by clinician, and the report mixed oral and local cromolyn routes. It therefore supplies direct MCAS-labelled exposure while remaining unable to establish an attributable outcome.

The randomized evidence remains a small withdrawal trial in systemic mastocytosis rather than MCAS. Oral cromolyn is minimally absorbed from the gut, while its US label covers mastocytosis and describes both gastrointestinal and non-gastrointestinal manifestations. The label does not carry a mast cell activation syndrome indication; use in MCAS is off-label.
