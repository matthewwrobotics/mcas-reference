Cromolyn sodium inhibits the release of preformed mediators from mast cell granules, and is the compound from which the "mast cell stabiliser" class takes its name — it descends from khellin, isolated from *Ammi visnaga*. The precise molecular target remains contested; reviews of the class note that cromolyn's stabilising effect was characterised clinically well before its mechanism was, and that several proposed targets have not been settled.

Oral cromolyn is minimally absorbed from the gut, which is why its labelled use is directed at gastrointestinal rather than systemic mast cell involvement. Its US label covers mastocytosis, not mast cell activation syndrome; use in MCAS is off-label.
