Benzodiazepines are a drug class rather than a single mast-cell intervention. Their established pharmacology is positive allosteric modulation of GABA-A receptors in the nervous system. The current lorazepam-tablet label supplies the representative US approval and safety boundary on this page; it does not establish that lorazepam shares every mast-cell finding reported for another compound in the class.

In the multicenter MCAS questionnaire, 324 participants reported present or past benzodiazepine exposure and supplied retrospective health ratings and adverse-effect reports. The questionnaire did not capture the individual agents, formulations, monotherapy status, or prescribing clinician. A recorded first-party discussion separately captures Leonard B. Weinstock describing his own use of lorazepam or midazolam in selected severe or hospital contexts. That supports the documented-specialist-use tag, while the survey remains the direct patient evidence.

The laboratory literature is mixed and compound-specific. Diazepam, flunitrazepam, clonazepam, and 4-chlorodiazepam were examined in human mast-cell lines or isolated human mast cells, but lorazepam was not. One study found only modest activity for clonazepam, another found clonazepam inactive while the research ligand 4-chlorodiazepam changed several activation pathways, and an older human-tissue experiment observed histamine release with midazolam. These findings support a research rationale for selected compounds, not a uniform class effect or a direct lorazepam mast-cell mechanism.
