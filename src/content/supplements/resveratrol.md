Resveratrol is a stilbene polyphenol found in grapes and sold as a dietary supplement. A laboratory study using mature human mast cells isolated from intestinal tissue examined FcεRI cross-linking. Under those conditions, resveratrol reduced degranulation and several cytokine and chemokine expression signals alongside reduced ERK1/2 and STAT3 phosphorylation.

A separate study using primary human skin mast cells produced a more selective pattern. At lower experimental exposures, prostaglandin D2 production fell while degranulation did not, and TNF production increased. Only the highest tested exposure broadly suppressed the measured endpoints other than Syk phosphorylation.

Together, the studies show that resveratrol can interact with IgE-linked signalling in human mast cells in the laboratory. They also show why “mast cell stabiliser” would be too broad a label: the direction and size of the observed changes depended on the tissue, mediator, and experimental exposure. Neither study administered oral resveratrol to people with MCAS.
