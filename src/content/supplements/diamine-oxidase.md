Diamine oxidase is the enzyme responsible for degrading ingested histamine at the intestinal mucosa. The rationale for supplementing it is narrow and specific: in individuals with reduced intestinal DAO activity, orally administered enzyme is intended to act within the gut lumen on histamine from food, before it is absorbed.

Two boundaries are worth stating plainly. The proposed action is confined to dietary histamine in the gut — it is not a mechanism for histamine that mast cells release into tissue, which is what defines mast cell activation. And the supporting literature concerns histamine intolerance, a separate condition from MCAS, which is why this entry is filed under a mechanism rather than an indication.
